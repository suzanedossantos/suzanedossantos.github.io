Where Is My Persona? The limits of personas in evaluation with trans and gender diverse people

Comparison between GenderMag and the experience of a trans person creating a Reddit account. The risk that mattered most to her barely shows up in the method.

My role

Study supervisor, taking part in planning and in the evaluations.

Type
Qualitative research and method critique, in inclusive evaluation
Methods
GenderMag inspection, moderated inspection session with think-aloud and structured comparison of the two results
Product evaluated
Reddit sign-up flow
Team
Israel Amorim, Suzane Santos, Samantha Silva, Giselle Melo, Carolina Farias, Clarice Mendes, Kamila Rodrigues and Marcelle Mota (UFPA and USP)
Recognition
Best Paper Award, IHC 2026
Outcome
Presented at IHC 2026 (October), Innovative Ideas and Emerging Results Track. Proceedings forthcoming

Summary

GenderMag is a well-known method for finding "inclusivity bugs" with personas based on cognitive styles. But it starts from a binary view of gender. We compared what the method finds with what happens when a trans person creates an account on a social network. What weighed most for her, the safety of her own identity, barely appears in the method. That is why we proposed a new dimension, "Identity Safety Risk".

The problem

For trans and gender diverse people, social networks are spaces of support, community and identity exploration. They are also places of risk: exposure without consent, harassment and unwanted links to a previous identity. Product teams use personas to anticipate barriers, but what happens when the persona does not represent those who face the most risk?

Research question

Can GenderMag's cognitive facets, especially "Risk", capture the barriers that trans and gender diverse people face on social networks?

Process

1. Planning

We defined a realistic scenario in which revealing one's identity is an important decision: creating a Reddit account to look for information about gender transition and find communities.

2. GenderMag inspection

A team of four people walked through the sign-up flow with the persona "Abby", in the roles set by the method: two facilitators, one driver and one recorder.

3. Moderated session with a trans participant

  • The participant performed the same task in the lab, thinking aloud.
  • Before the session, the scenario was shared with her only to confirm she felt comfortable, since she was in social transition.
  • She did not know the study's hypotheses, so as not to be influenced.

4. Comparison

Each problem from both methods was placed at the step of the flow where it happened (username, interests, feed personalization), to see where the methods converged and where they diverged.

Bias control: except for the first author, no one took part in both evaluations.

Key findings

1. GenderMag found problems, but almost none related to risk

  • 21 questions answered in the inspection, 9 with "maybe" or "no", consolidated into 8 inclusivity bugs.
  • 4 of the 8 were related to information processing, such as long terms of use, no confirmation after validating the email and a pop-up that closed before it could be read.
  • Only 1 of the 8 was related to the Risk facet.

2. The participant experienced sign-up as constant risk management

Four behavior patterns emerged:

  • Exposing as little as possible: she skipped optional fields, chose email sign-up over Google login and declined to save her password, to avoid linking the new account to her existing digital identity.
  • Permanent username as a risk: when she found out the username cannot be changed, she feared that something chosen during her transition would be tied forever to who she was.
  • Confusion between display name and username: it was unclear what was permanent and what was public. For her, this was a barrier to protecting her identity, not just an interface question.
  • Restricting visibility as a precaution.

3. A discreet warning can have a high cost

The warning that the username is permanent was in small print. For most people, it is a detail. For a person in transition, it can mean lasting exposure.

What changed as a result

Finding Proposal
The "Risk" facet does not capture identity-related threats New exploratory dimension: "Identity Safety Risk", to be incorporated by InclusiveMag
Low-visibility warning about username permanence Highlight the warning before the name is chosen
Feed personalization creates distrust Explain that choices only affect one's own feed, are not public and can be changed
Feedback bugs in sign-up Clear confirmations, a plain-language summary of the terms of use and an option to skip interests

Limitations

  • A single participant and a single flow: the study is exploratory and the new dimension still needs to be validated with more people.
  • The participant was part of the research group, which was controlled by not revealing the study's goal and hypotheses to her.

What I learned

An inclusion method can also exclude. Personas help anticipate barriers, but only the ones that fit the categories they already have. Comparing an inspection with a real person's lived experience showed exactly where the method stopped seeing. It also reinforced the ethical care that research with vulnerable groups requires, starting from planning.
